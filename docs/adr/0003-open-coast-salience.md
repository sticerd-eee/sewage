# Coastal salience follows overflow proximity to the open coast

**Status: superseded** by [Restore original tidal-coast salience](../plans/2026-09-20-002-restore-tidal-coast-salience-plan.md).
The decision below is retained as historical context. The active definition uses
the original tidal Mean High Water coastline and excludes London properties.

The original tidal Mean High Water measure follows estuarine and tidal-river
shores, placing parts of London near the measured coastline. Coastal salience
will instead use overflow distance to actual shoreline adjoining coastal water,
including coastal bays and excluding estuarine and tidal-river shores. Receiving
water type is not an eligibility restriction: an overflow near the open coast
can qualify even if it discharges into an estuary.

This preserves the interpretation of coastal salience as a geographic
characteristic of the discharge location. London becomes eligible under the
same classification as other places; exclusion of London remains a robustness
check. Preserve the prior tidal measurement for provenance instead of silently
changing the meaning of historical results.

These domain choices were confirmed in the 19 September 2026 interview.
Implementation and report-only checks are described in the
[open-coast salience plan](../plans/2026-09-19-2209-fix-open-coast-salience-plan.md).
The [refinement decision record](../plans/2026-09-19-001-salience-dataset-refinement-discussion.md)
preserves the supporting evidence and rationale.
