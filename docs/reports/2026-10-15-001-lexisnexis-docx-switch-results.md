# LexisNexis search 1: switch from the PDF to the DOCX article data — 15 October 2026

The search 1 article data now come from the DOCX exports cleaned by `scripts/R/02_data_cleaning/clean_lexis_nexis_search1.R`. That script was written by Ana Werneck (sticerd-eee/sewage#24) and replaces the PDF pipeline. It writes the same two files as before, `search1_articles.parquet` and `search1_monthly.parquet`. The analysis scripts are therefore unchanged, but every result that uses the article counts moves.

The DOCX corpus has 1,399 articles, against 1,322 from the PDFs, and runs to July 2026 rather than July 2025. Every analysis stops in 2024, so the extension never enters an estimate. Over 2014–2024 the two monthly series have a correlation of 0.993. The new series is 5–9 per cent lower in 2021–2024 (for example, 246 rather than 260 articles in 2023). The August 2022 peak falls from 54 to 46 articles.

## Method

Every script that reads the article counts was run twice with the same code: once on the PDF-era data and once on the DOCX-era data. The code was `main` after #41 and #42, plus this branch's switch of `did_attention_repeat_extensive.R` to `spatial_vcov()`. The windowed scripts ran before their "prior" twins, as in the 27 September Overleaf sync, because both write four of the same table files.

The PDF-era run reproduced every existing table in `output/tables/` byte for byte. So the differences below come only from the article data.

The switch of `did_attention_repeat_extensive.R` to the shared helper left its table byte-identical.

Scripts run (16):
- `01_descriptive/google_trends_article_counts_combined.R`
- In `05_news/`:
  - `did_articles_lag4_prior.R` and `did_articles_lag4_prior_extensive.R`
  - `did_articles_lagged_sales.R` and `did_articles_lagged_sales_extensive.R`
  - `summarise_lagged_attention_sales.R` and `plot_lagged_attention_extensive_slides.R`
  - `did_articles_windowed_prior.R`, `did_articles_prior.R` and `did_articles_prior_salience.R`
  - `did_articles_windowed_prior_extensive.R`, `did_articles_prior_extensive.R` and `did_articles_prior_extensive_salience.R`
  - `extensive_margin_coefficient_plots.R`
  - `did_attention_repeat_extensive.R`

## All tables

There are 47 tables. Three are identical. In the other 44, 460 of 1,176 coefficient cells move in at least one printed digit of the estimate or its SE, and 37 cells change significance level.
- **Windowed-article tables:** 33 of the 37 star changes are here, with 422 of 968 cells changed. Windowed counts sum only the most recent 3 to 12 months, where the two series differ most. Apart from the slide table, none of these is used in the paper or decks.
- **The other tables:** 38 of 208 cells change and 4 change stars. Two of those are listed above. The other two are `did_articles_lag4_prior` (rentals column 6, log articles at a four-month lag, 0.020*** → 0.020**) and `did_articles_prior_1000m` (column 6, the same cell as the radius-robustness change).

In the lagged-sales effect sizes, all 28 article-based estimates move, by at most 11.5 per cent. One crosses a threshold: intensive sales, 1,000 m, lag 0, full reference sample, p = 0.052 → 0.047. The 23 Post-indicator estimates are unchanged.

## Wording affected

The note to the paper figure `fig:news-desc` (`02_background_context.tex`) says the right axis is scaled so that "the maximum article count (54)" aligns with the Google Trends maximum. With the new data the maximum is 46.
